Methodology

How mission coordinates are documented

STEP 01

Orbital survey verification

Cameras aboard the Lunar Reconnaissance Orbiter and Mars Reconnaissance Orbiter photograph surface hardware directly from orbit.

STEP 02

Telemetry triangulation

Doppler shifts in radio transmissions record the precise impact or soft touchdown latitude and longitude.

STEP 03

Preservation under accords

Landing locations are cataloged as protected heritage zones under international space agreements.

Questions & Context

Frequently addressed inquiries

Why does NASA leave equipment behind?

Returning machines to Earth requires heavy fuel and propulsion systems. Leaving descent stages and science stations behind allows space probes to carry more scientific instruments.

Is any hardware still functional today?

Most are completely unpowered. However, retroreflectors left on the Moon by Apollo crews still bounce laser beams back to Earth observatories today to measure lunar orbit drift.

Are these items legally protected?

Yes. International frameworks and the Artemis Accords designate key sites, such as Apollo 11 Tranquility Base, as protected archaeological zones of human heritage.

What caused Opportunity rover to shut down?

In June 2018, a planet circling dust storm blocked solar light, exhausting Opportunity batteries after fourteen years of travel across Mars.
